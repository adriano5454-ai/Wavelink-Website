Offshore equipment inventory

A packing list says what was meant to travel. An inventory should also tell the next person which unit to look for, where to find it and what was checked.

Identify the unit, not just the model

Two instruments may share a part number but carry different serial numbers. Keep the unit’s identity in its equipment record, the responsible department in its own field, and the physical location separate. A survey instrument can belong to Survey while sitting on a workshop shelf.

Bring the shore list aboard

Start from an inventory worksheet when the equipment already exists in a spreadsheet. Select a sheet, review how its headers map to fields such as serial number and quantity, then correct the draft before importing. A quantity on the worksheet is a starting record, not evidence that someone found the item.

Check a box or a defined area

Choose the scope of the stock check and use printed item QR codes where they are available. For a spares box, inspect the contents rather than treating the box label as proof that every part is inside. Look up the item’s recorded location and associated maintenance or certificate records when a discrepancy needs context.

Before mobilisation

A technician finds two identical instruments on the bench. Their part numbers match; only one serial number is on the packing list. Checking the equipment records identifies the intended unit before the shore team lists it on the outbound manifest.
