Offshore shift and hitch handovers

The relief crew needs to know what changed, what remains open and which record to consult first. A department handover preserves that context when a watch ends or an entire crew rotates.

Choose a daily or hitch handover

At a daily change, focus on what the incoming shift needs in its first hours. For a hitch change, also explain decisions made during the rotation and which items still need attention. Use the relevant department and configured shifts so the record is addressed to the right team.

Write the next action while the details are fresh

Continue a saved draft as work progresses. Instead of writing “shipment received”, say what has arrived, where it was last seen, what remains to be done and which equipment or manifest identifier the relief crew should look up. Separate what was observed from what still needs confirmation.

Publish for acknowledgement

Publish the handover when the outgoing team is finished editing. The incoming person can acknowledge the published handover. Acknowledgement documents that handover step; it should not be mistaken for completing the operational work described in it.

An actionable watch change

“The spares box is in the receiving area. Confirm its contents against the manifest before moving it to the workshop.” The incoming shift can act on that note without assuming placement is already complete.
