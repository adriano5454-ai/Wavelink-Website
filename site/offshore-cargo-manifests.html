Offshore cargo manifests and receiving

“Dispatched”, “received” and “placed” answer three different questions. Use the manifest for the shipment, then record where the equipment was put so the receiving crew can find it.

At the shore base: prepare and dispatch

Identify the equipment or boxes going to the vessel. Check the unit or box identifiers against what is being loaded, prepare the shipment record and record dispatch when it leaves. Dispatch tells the vessel what to expect; it does not confirm that the cargo is on board.

At the vessel: confirm receipt

Compare the arriving equipment with the manifest. Record receipt for what the team can confirm. A box in the receiving area may be on board but not yet available in the workshop. Keep that distinction clear for the people planning the next shift.

After receipt: record final placement

Record where the received equipment is placed. The manifest describes a particular movement; the inventory record is where the team can look for the equipment’s recorded location later. When a shift ends before placement, carry that outstanding action into the handover.

One box, two different answers

A spares box has arrived at the vessel. The deck team can confirm delivery, but a technician still needs to know where to collect it. Recording the final location after the box moves from receiving to the equipment bay answers both questions without treating them as the same event.
